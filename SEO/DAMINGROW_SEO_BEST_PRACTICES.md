## 1. Purpose and authority

These rules are the default operating standard whenever anyone researches, plans, writes, edits, publishes, migrates, or technically optimises D-AminGrow pages.

They apply to:
- Homepage and company pages.
- Product categories and individual product pages.
- Day-old chick and point-of-lay pages and booking journeys.
- Livestock vaccines, veterinary drugs, supplements, and equipment pages.
- Consultancy and technical service landing pages.
- Educational articles, guides, explainers, and resources.
- Poultry calculators and interactive tools.
- Location and country pages.
- Metadata, structured data, internal links, images, videos, and sitemaps.

**SEO exists to help the right people discover useful, accurate D-AminGrow information and services. Rankings are a means, not the end.**

Follow current Google Search Essentials, current Google Search Central documentation, applicable laws and regulations, and relevant professional veterinary guidance. If this document conflicts with current official guidance or applicable law, follow the current authoritative requirement and update this document.

No AI agent or developer may invent business facts, credentials, stock, availability, prices, service coverage, regulatory approvals, or customer outcomes.

## 3. Non-negotiable people-first principles

Every indexable page must have a clear audience, purpose, and useful outcome.

- **People-first:** Write for farmers, poultry entrepreneurs, livestock operators, agricultural professionals, and buyers—not search engines.
- **Search-intent satisfaction:** Address the visitor's main question or task near the beginning.
- **Original value:** Add practical explanations, verified operational knowledge, original examples, diagrams, checklists, calculators, photos, or expert analysis.
- **Accuracy:** Verify technical, commercial, regulatory, and veterinary claims before publication.
- **Experience and expertise:** Make the real author, reviewer, qualifications, and relevant experience clear where appropriate.
- **Trust:** Publish genuine business details, clear contact methods, transparent service limitations, and authentic evidence.
- **Completeness:** Cover the subject sufficiently for the reader's task; do not write to an arbitrary word count.
- **Usability:** Make content easy to scan, understand, navigate, and use on mobile devices.
- **Accessibility:** Use semantic HTML, meaningful link text, keyboard-accessible controls, readable contrast, and descriptive image alternatives.
- **Freshness:** Review information when regulations, product details, professional guidance, market conditions, or business operations change.

Google's E-E-A-T concepts (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) are quality concepts, not a single score or standalone ranking factor. Trust is especially important. An author box or reviewer badge does not automatically improve rankings.

### The “Who, How, Why” test

Before publishing, answer:
- **Who:** Who created this content? Who reviewed it? Are their identities and credentials real?
- **How:** What sources, experience, tests, observations, calculations, or editorial process support it?
- **Why:** What real user need does it satisfy beyond attracting search traffic?

If these questions cannot be answered honestly, do not publish.

## 4. Strict prohibitions

Zero tolerance for:
- Keyword stuffing, hidden text, hidden links, or unnatural repetition.
- Doorway pages and near-identical location pages created only to rank.
- Mass-produced AI articles with little original value or human review.
- Rewriting competitor articles with synonyms and presenting them as original research.
- Scraped, copied, spun, or plagiarised content.
- Fake authors, fabricated veterinary reviewers, invented qualifications, or AI-generated staff identities.
- Fake testimonials, reviews, customer logos, case studies, farm photographs, or endorsements.
- Fabricated statistics, success rates, customer counts, delivery coverage, search volumes, traffic, or market share.
- Claims of NAFDAC approval, registration, certification, or product authorisation without documentary verification for the exact product and context.
- Unsupported disease-treatment, prevention, cure, safety, efficacy, or dosage claims.
- Unverified product availability, hatchery stock, dispatch dates, bird counts, or delivery promises.
- Invented fixed product prices or stale prices presented as current.
- Misleading structured data or schema content not visible and accurate on the page.
- Fake FAQ content or FAQ markup intended to manipulate search appearance.
- Buying links, automated link schemes, private blog networks, or manipulative link exchanges.
- Cloaking, sneaky redirects, hacked content, or misleading functionality.
- Automatically publishing AI output without editorial and subject-matter checks.
- Creating pages solely because a keyword tool reports high volume.
- Promising first position, guaranteed indexing, guaranteed traffic, or guaranteed 500,000 monthly visits.

AI assistance is allowed for research support, outlining, editing, translation, and drafting. It does not replace fact-checking, originality, professional review, or editorial responsibility. Scaled content abuse can violate Google's spam policies regardless of whether content is generated by AI or humans.

## 5. Keyword research and search intent

Keyword research must begin with customer problems and business relevance.

### Research sources

Use and record real data from:
- Google Search Console: actual queries, impressions, clicks, CTR, and positions.
- Google Keyword Planner: keyword ideas and advertising-planning estimates.
- Google Trends: relative interest and seasonality, not absolute search volume.
- Google autocomplete and related searches: language and query discovery.
- Reputable SEO tools such as Ahrefs, Semrush, or Ubersuggest, when available.
- Customer enquiries, WhatsApp questions, sales conversations, support questions, and staff expertise.
- Competitor search results: identify gaps and user expectations; do not copy competitor content.

Record the tool, date, country, language, network, and metric definition. Distinguish exact data from estimates. Never invent volume, keyword difficulty, CPC, traffic potential, or ranking difficulty.

### Search intent classification

Classify each target query as one or more of:
- **Informational:** Learn how something works or solve a farming problem.
- **Commercial investigation:** Compare products, breeds, services, suppliers, or approaches.
- **Transactional/contact intent:** Book chicks, request current price, order supplies, or request a consultation.
- **Navigational/branded:** Find D-AminGrow, a named service, or a known resource.
- **Local intent:** Find a provider or service in a particular real service area.

The page format and CTA must match the dominant intent.

## 6. Page titles, meta descriptions, URLs, and headings

### SEO title
- Write a unique, descriptive title that accurately summarises the page.
- Include the primary topic naturally when useful.
- Put distinctive information early when it improves clarity.
- Use the brand name where appropriate, often near the end.
- Avoid repetitive templates that make many pages look identical.
- Keep titles concise enough to reduce truncation, but do not treat 60 characters as a Google ranking rule or hard limit.
- Do not promise benefits, stock, prices, or credentials the page cannot support.

### Meta description
- Write a unique, accurate summary.
- State the main user benefit and, where appropriate, the next step.
- Include the primary topic naturally if it reads well.
- Approximately 140–160 characters can be a useful editorial target, not a fixed Google limit.
- Do not stuff keywords or duplicate descriptions across the site.
- Google may generate a different snippet based on the query and page content. A meta description is not a guaranteed snippet or ranking guarantee.

### URL slug
- Use lowercase words separated by hyphens.
- Keep URLs short, descriptive, stable, and understandable.
- Avoid unnecessary dates, IDs, tracking parameters, and filler words.
- Do not change established URLs without a migration reason and redirect plan.
- Use a consistent trailing-slash policy.
- Do not force an exact-match keyword into an awkward URL.

### Headings
- Use one clear, descriptive H1 as a practical editorial convention.
- Organise sections with logical H2 and H3 headings.
- Use headings to describe the content that follows, not as keyword containers.
- Include keyword variants only where they naturally describe a section.
- Do not skip heading levels for visual styling; use CSS for appearance.
- Do not promise answers the page does not provide.

### Opening section

The opening should:
1. Confirm the visitor is on the right page.
2. Address the primary question, product, service, or task.
3. Explain the useful next step.
4. Avoid long generic introductions and unsupported superlatives.

## 7. Content standards by page type

### Homepage

Explain what D-AminGrow does, who it serves, its main product and service categories, how customers request current prices or make bookings, how to contact the business, and what makes its approach useful using only verifiable differentiators. Link prominently to core product, booking, consultancy, and knowledge sections.

### Product category pages

Include a clear category introduction, relevant subcategories and product links, safe and accurate product context, verified selection/handling considerations, availability and enquiry instructions, related services, educational guides, and genuine FAQs. Avoid thin pages containing only a heading and product grid.

### Individual product pages

Include only verified information:
- Exact product name and brand/manufacturer where authorised.
- Product category and intended species/use where supported by approved product information.
- Active ingredient or composition only where verified and appropriate.
- Pack size and formulation only when confirmed.
- Manufacturer instructions and approved label information where applicable.
- Storage and handling requirements from authoritative product documentation.
- Regulatory registration/approval details only after checking the exact product and jurisdiction.
- Current availability only if inventory data is reliable.
- Clear “Request Current Market Price” or “Enquire About This Product” CTA.
- Related products or educational resources where relevant.

**D-AminGrow pricing rule:** Do not publish a fabricated or stale fixed price. Do not output `Product`/`Offer` structured-data prices when no current, genuine public price exists. Never use a fake zero price, placeholder price, or “₦1” price to satisfy schema requirements. Use non-price product information only when supported by the relevant Google and Schema.org requirements.

A product page must not imply approval, stock, suitability for every flock, or guaranteed health outcomes unless substantiated.

### Day-old chick and point-of-lay pages

Explain bird type and breed/strain where confirmed, intended production purpose, known source/hatchery, actual booking process, dispatch/collection/delivery arrangements only when confirmed, applicable packaging/minimum terms, arrival preparation, price/availability confirmation, and booking enquiry steps.

Never imply live availability if the website is not connected to reliable current inventory or hatchery quota data. Label provisional schedules clearly.

### Service and consultancy pages

Answer:
- What problem does the service address?
- Who is it for?
- What is included and excluded?
- What information should the customer prepare?
- Is it remote, on-site, or both?
- What geographic area is actually served?
- What happens after an enquiry?
- What experience, qualifications, and evidence support the service?
- How can the visitor request assistance?

Use relevant CTAs such as “Request Farm Consultation” or “Speak to the Technical Team.” Do not invent response-time guarantees.

### Educational articles and guides

Every article should:
- Have a clear reader and purpose.
- Answer the main question early.
- Explain practical steps, context, limitations, and common mistakes.
- Distinguish established guidance from opinion or local practice.
- Cite authoritative sources for technical and regulatory claims.
- Include original examples, checklists, illustrations, or observations where possible.
- Link to genuinely relevant D-AminGrow tools, products, or services.
- State the author and qualified reviewer where appropriate.
- Show publication and meaningful review/update dates.
- Be corrected or retired when guidance becomes unsafe or obsolete.

Do not pad articles to reach a target word count. No universal word count guarantees rankings.

### Calculators and interactive tools

For each tool:
- Explain its purpose and intended user.
- Display input assumptions and units.
- Explain the calculation method sufficiently for users to interpret results.
- Cite authoritative sources for technical assumptions.
- Show limitations and when professional advice is required.
- Make controls keyboard accessible and mobile friendly.
- Provide helpful error handling.
- Do not present estimates as guaranteed outcomes.
- Test calculations with known examples and edge cases.
- Keep important explanatory content in crawlable HTML.

Vaccination planners and health-related tools must use professionally reviewed schedules and clearly state that the output is a planning aid, not an individual veterinary prescription or diagnosis.

### Author, reviewer, and about pages

Publish genuine profiles only. Include relevant background, professional qualifications, affiliations, and responsibilities when verified and permitted. Do not use fabricated credentials, stock headshots presented as real staff, or misleading titles.

## 8. Veterinary, animal-health, and regulatory content safeguards

Animal-health content requires heightened editorial care because incorrect information can harm animals, farms, livelihoods, and food systems.

### Mandatory review

Content about disease, symptoms, vaccination, medicines, antibiotics, dosage, treatment, withdrawal periods, contraindications, storage, or product safety must be reviewed by a suitably qualified and authorised veterinary professional or other competent subject-matter expert, as appropriate.

### Mandatory safeguards

- Use current, reputable veterinary and government sources.
- Prefer product labels, official registration information, recognised veterinary authorities, peer-reviewed research, and established professional guidance.
- Record source title, publisher, URL, publication/update date, and date accessed.
- Distinguish general education from diagnosis or treatment.
- Do not recommend a drug, antibiotic, vaccine, dose, route, schedule, or withdrawal period without appropriate professional and product-specific support.
- Do not encourage misuse of antibiotics or other veterinary medicines.
- Do not present a symptom list as a definitive disease diagnosis.
- Include urgent escalation advice where animal welfare or a potentially serious outbreak is involved.
- Do not imply a calculator or chatbot replaces a veterinarian.
- Do not make unsupported claims that a product prevents, treats, cures, or eliminates a disease.
- Check applicable rules for each country before publishing country-specific regulatory guidance.

### Nigeria-specific verification

For Nigerian veterinary products and claims:
- Check current NAFDAC requirements and official product registration information.
- Verify the exact product, manufacturer, formulation, registration status, and permitted claims.
- Do not assume one product's registration applies to another formulation or pack.
- Do not advertise a product as NAFDAC-approved based solely on a supplier statement or old certificate image.
- Keep evidence and date of verification in the internal product record.

Where information is uncertain, say so and direct readers to a qualified professional or relevant authority.

- Avoid orphan pages.
- Use HTML links crawlers can discover.
- Keep navigation understandable and consistent.
- Add breadcrumbs where they help users understand the hierarchy.
- Do not create links simply to increase keyword density.

## 10. Image, video, and media SEO

### Images
- Use genuine D-AminGrow product, farm, team, equipment, and operational photographs whenever possible.
- Do not present stock imagery as D-AminGrow's own farm, staff, customers, or inventory.
- Use descriptive file names, e.g. `broiler-day-old-chicks-hatchery.jpg`.
- Use modern formats such as AVIF or WebP where supported and appropriate.
- Provide responsive image sizes; avoid oversized originals on mobile.
- Compress images without visibly damaging product or technical detail.
- Set image dimensions or aspect ratios to reduce layout shift.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images; do not unnecessarily lazy-load the primary above-the-fold/LCP image.
- Use descriptive alt text for informative images.
- Use empty alt text (`alt=""`) for purely decorative images.
- Do not stuff exact-match keywords into alt text.
- Ensure product photos accurately represent the actual product and packaging.
- Respect image ownership, licensing, and usage permissions.

### Video
- Give useful videos descriptive titles and context.
- Provide captions or transcripts where practical.
- Use a stable, crawlable video landing page when video search visibility is a goal.
- Do not autoplay intrusive audio.
- Do not use video markup for videos not meaningfully present on the page.
- Ensure the page remains useful if the video does not load.

## 11. Technical SEO requirements

Verify the implementation against the actual Next.js application and Cloudflare deployment. Do not assume a configuration works because it exists in a file.

### Rendering and crawlability
- Important public content should be present in initial HTML through server rendering or static generation where appropriate.
- Do not make essential text, navigation, product information, or internal links dependent solely on delayed client-side interactions.
- Use semantic HTML and crawlable anchor links.
- Confirm Google can render the final page and see its primary content.
- Check that API errors do not produce empty or misleading indexable pages.
- Keep private admin and customer data inaccessible to crawlers.

### HTTP status and redirects
- Return `200` for valid pages.
- Return real `404` or appropriate `410` responses for removed pages where applicable.
- Use permanent redirects for genuinely permanent URL changes.
- Avoid redirect chains and loops.
- Redirect old URLs to the closest relevant replacement, not automatically to the homepage.
- Do not redirect unrelated or unavailable pages to misleading destinations.
- Update internal links, canonicals, and sitemaps after migrations.
- Maintain a tested redirect map for every URL migration.

### Canonical URLs
- Every indexable page should have one correct canonical URL.
- Use absolute canonicals on the preferred HTTPS hostname.
- Ensure canonicals agree with redirects and sitemap URLs.
- Do not canonicalise distinct, useful pages to the homepage.
- Resolve duplicate query-string, trailing-slash, case, and parameter variations deliberately.
- Do not generate multiple or conflicting canonical tags.

### XML sitemap
- Include only canonical, indexable, successful public URLs.
- Exclude admin routes, private pages, API endpoints, redirects, error pages, and `noindex` URLs.
- Include accurate `lastmod` values only when the page has materially changed.
- Split sitemaps when appropriate and use a sitemap index for larger sites.
- Ensure sitemap URLs use the preferred hostname and HTTPS.
- Submit the sitemap in Google Search Console.
- Sitemap submission helps discovery; it does not guarantee crawling, indexing, or rankings.

### Robots directives
- Maintain a valid `robots.txt`.
- Do not block resources required for rendering.
- Do not use `robots.txt` as access control or as a reliable deindexing method.
- Use `noindex` for pages that should not appear in search, while ensuring crawlers can access the directive.
- Protect private content with authentication and authorisation, not SEO directives.

### Metadata and social sharing
- Provide unique page titles and descriptions.
- Set appropriate Open Graph title, description, URL, and image.
- Use suitable social image dimensions and verify previews.
- Use a consistent brand name and logo.
- Ensure metadata reflects visible page content.

### Mobile experience
- Design and test mobile-first.
- Keep text readable without zooming.
- Make booking and enquiry forms easy on small screens.
- Ensure buttons and tap targets are usable.
- Avoid intrusive popups and interstitials.
- Test on realistic low-bandwidth connections and lower-powered devices common in target markets.

### Core Web Vitals and performance

Use Google's current “good” field thresholds at the 75th percentile:
- **LCP:** 2.5 seconds or less.
- **INP:** 200 milliseconds or less.
- **CLS:** 0.1 or less.

Measure real-user field data where available and use lab tools for diagnosis. A passing Lighthouse score does not guarantee good field performance.

Optimise server response and caching, image delivery, font loading, JavaScript bundle size, third-party scripts, client-side hydration, database/API latency, layout stability, and long tasks. Do not sacrifice accessibility, security, or useful functionality merely to improve a synthetic score.

## 12. Structured data and Schema.org

Structured data must describe visible, accurate page content. It can help search engines understand entities and pages, but does not guarantee rich results or higher rankings.

Use JSON-LD where appropriate and maintain it from the same trusted data source as visible content.

### Potential schema types

Use only when the page and business genuinely qualify:
- `Organization` — accurate company identity and official contact information.
- The most specific appropriate `LocalBusiness` subtype — only if D-AminGrow has a real-world local business presence and accurate details.
- `BreadcrumbList` — visible, accurate breadcrumb hierarchy.
- `Article` or `BlogPosting` — genuine editorial articles with accurate author, dates, and publisher.
- `Person` — real authors or reviewers with accurate identity and profile details.
- `Product` — actual individual product pages with accurate product identity and supported properties.
- `Service` — may describe a genuine service using Schema.org vocabulary, but do not assume it creates a Google rich result.
- `VideoObject` — only for a real, accessible video page with accurate metadata.
- `WebSite` — accurate site identity and name where useful.

### Product pricing rule

D-AminGrow uses current-market-price-on-request:
- Do not invent `Offer.price`.
- Do not use zero, one naira, placeholder, or stale values.
- Do not mark a product `InStock` unless actual availability data supports the claim.
- Do not add review or aggregate-rating markup without genuine qualifying reviews and compliance with Google's policies.
- Do not implement merchant listing markup as if customers can purchase at a displayed fixed price when they cannot.
- Review current Google Product structured-data documentation before implementation.

If a page cannot satisfy the requirements for a Google search feature, omit that feature's markup rather than misrepresent the page.

### FAQ markup

FAQs can still be useful visible content for readers. Do not promise FAQ rich results. Google's FAQ rich-result visibility has been restricted primarily to well-known, authoritative government and health websites. Implement FAQ structured data only if current Google eligibility and policy requirements are met.

### Validation
- Validate JSON-LD syntax.
- Use Schema.org Validator and Google's Rich Results Test where relevant.
- Check required fields and policy violations.
- Compare structured data with visible page content.
- Test after every template or CMS change.
- Monitor Search Console enhancement reports where applicable.

## 14. Editorial governance and CMS requirements

The CMS should support:
- Draft, review, approved, scheduled, published, archived, and withdrawn states.
- Author and reviewer attribution.
- Content classification and risk level.
- Reference/source records.
- Publication and last meaningful review dates.
- SEO title, description, slug, canonical, robots directive, and social metadata.
- Image alt text and media credits.
- Internal links and related resources.
- Redirect management.
- Editorial notes and revision history.
- Content expiry or review reminders.
- Approval restrictions for veterinary and regulatory content.

### Editorial workflow
1. Research the user problem and search intent.
2. Confirm a new page is needed and does not duplicate an existing page.
3. Gather reliable sources and first-hand information.
4. Create a content brief and target-page mapping.
5. Draft original content.
6. Fact-check all claims and calculations.
7. Obtain subject-matter review where required.
8. Edit for clarity, usefulness, accessibility, and brand voice.
9. Review metadata, internal links, images, and structured data.
10. Run technical and editorial checks.
11. Publish only after approval.
12. Monitor performance and update when needed.

AI-generated drafts must remain unpublished until required human and expert review is complete.

## 16. Conversion SEO and enquiry measurement

SEO success must connect to real business outcomes.

### Primary conversion actions
- WhatsApp product enquiry.
- Request current market price.
- Day-old chick booking enquiry.
- Point-of-lay bird enquiry.
- Farm consultancy request.
- Technical service request.
- Contact form submission.
- Relevant resource download or calculator completion, where meaningful.

### Measurement requirements

Track events using a privacy-conscious analytics implementation:
- `view_item` or a suitable product-view event.
- `click_whatsapp`.
- `request_price`.
- `chick_booking_start`.
- `chick_booking_submit`.
- `point_of_lay_enquiry`.
- `consultation_request`.
- `technical_service_request`.
- `contact_submit`.
- `calculator_complete`.

Event names may be adapted to the analytics platform, but definitions must be documented and consistent.

Capture UTM parameters and original landing-page attribution where lawful and technically reliable. Do not send personal, health, or other sensitive customer data to analytics platforms.

Distinguish clicks from submitted enquiries, submitted enquiries from qualified leads, qualified leads from confirmed bookings/orders, and confirmed bookings from completed fulfilment.

WhatsApp link clicks do not prove a sale. Do not report them as revenue or completed orders.

### Reporting

Use Google Search Console for search performance and a suitable analytics/CRM source for onsite and business outcomes.

## 17. International and African expansion

Do not target every African country before D-AminGrow can serve them.

For each expansion market:
- Verify product and veterinary regulatory requirements.
- Confirm service and delivery capability.
- Research local search language and terminology.
- Check currency, logistics, payment, and customer-support realities.
- Create genuinely local content rather than replacing country names.
- Use country-specific pages only when there is sufficient original and useful information.
- Implement `hreflang` only when equivalent language or regional page variants genuinely exist and annotations are reciprocal and correct.
- Keep one canonical URL per intended page variant.

## 18. SEO quality assurance checklist

Complete this checklist before publishing or materially updating an indexable page.

### Strategy and content
- [ ] The page serves a real D-AminGrow audience and business purpose.
- [ ] Search intent is documented.
- [ ] The target page is not duplicating another URL.
- [ ] The page provides original, useful information.
- [ ] Claims and facts have been verified.
- [ ] Veterinary/regulatory content has appropriate expert review.
- [ ] Author and reviewer details are genuine.
- [ ] No unsupported statistics, credentials, or guarantees appear.

### On-page SEO
- [ ] Unique, descriptive title.
- [ ] Unique, useful meta description.
- [ ] Clean, stable URL.
- [ ] Clear H1 and logical heading hierarchy.
- [ ] Main question or purpose addressed early.
- [ ] Natural terminology; no keyword stuffing.
- [ ] Helpful internal links.
- [ ] Relevant external sources cited where needed.
- [ ] Clear, relevant CTA.
- [ ] Product prices and availability are accurate and follow the price-on-request policy.

### Media and accessibility
- [ ] Images are genuine, licensed, or accurately identified as illustrative.
- [ ] Informative images have descriptive alt text.
- [ ] Decorative images use empty alt text.
- [ ] Images are responsive and appropriately compressed.
- [ ] Layout dimensions minimise CLS.
- [ ] Video captions/transcripts are provided where practical.
- [ ] Keyboard and mobile usability checked.

### Technical SEO
- [ ] Page returns the correct HTTP status.
- [ ] Page is indexable when intended.
- [ ] Canonical is correct and unique.
- [ ] URL is in the sitemap if indexable.
- [ ] Internal links are crawlable.
- [ ] No redirect loop or chain.
- [ ] Metadata is present in rendered HTML.
- [ ] Structured data is valid and truthful, if used.
- [ ] No private information is exposed.
- [ ] Mobile layout and performance checked.

### Conversion and measurement
- [ ] WhatsApp and enquiry links work.
- [ ] Forms submit to the real backend and show errors clearly.
- [ ] Conversion events are tested.
- [ ] UTM attribution is preserved where appropriate.
- [ ] No sensitive customer information is sent to analytics.
- [ ] CRM receives the enquiry if the workflow requires it.

A page that fails a critical accuracy, safety, security, or functionality check must not be published merely to meet a schedule.
